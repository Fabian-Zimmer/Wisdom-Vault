## Recommended Equipment
- 2 rimmed baking sheets — for roasting the mushrooms
- Stand blender — for the broth; or immersion blender
- Medium or large saucepan — for the noodles and broth

## Instructions
**Notes:**
- Use a mix of mushroom varieties for best texture and flavour — oyster get the crispiest, shiitakes add meaty umami depth.
- Avoid lining sheet pans with parchment — the mushrooms won't get as crispy. Brush with oil directly. Just make sure the oven sheet pans are clean.
- The thinner you tear/slice the mushrooms, the crispier they get — but watch them closely as thin ones burn fast.
- Soy milk thickens significantly when heated; if the broth gets too thick, whisk in more mushroom or vegetable broth.
- Multitask: prep and roast mushrooms first, then cook noodles and make the broth while mushrooms are in the oven.
- Store broth and mushrooms separately in the fridge. Reheat broth over medium-low, whisking. Re-crisp mushrooms in a dry hot pan. Cook noodles fresh each time.

### Crispy Mushrooms
2. Preheat oven to 220°C (or later after prepping mushrooms if oven heat up quickly) with one rack in the top third and one in the bottom. Brush two rimmed baking sheets with some oil.
   *If only 1 oven sheet pan available:* use half amount for each step below and do 2 oven rounds.
3. Prep mushrooms (450-570g): for soft varieties (oyster, maitake), slice off tough stems and tear into thin strips with your hands. For sturdier varieties (shiitake, cremini), slice thinly. For beech mushrooms, leave whole and just trim the ends.
4. Divide mushrooms across both pans. Drizzle with oil (2 tbsp total), toss to coat well — add a little more oil if they seem dry. Season with salt (3/4 tsp) and toss again. Spread into a single layer, not overlapping.
5. Roast for 15 min. Remove from oven, stir and toss them around, then roast for a further 10 min. until deeply browned and crispy. Set aside.

### Noodles
5. While the mushrooms roast, bring a saucepan of water to a boil. Cook noodles according to the package. Drain and drizzle with a little toasted sesame oil to prevent sticking. Set aside. Return the same saucepan to the stove.

### Broth
6. Heat a frying pan over medium heat with oil (1 tbsp). Once hot, add garlic (6 cloves), ginger (1.5 inch piece), scallion whites and light greens, and chilli peppers (2-4). Cook, stirring frequently, for 3-4 min. until fragrant and lightly coloured. If there are lots of browned bits, deglaze with a splash of water. Transfer to a stand blender.
7. To the blender add mushroom or vegetable broth (240ml / 1 cup), soy milk (480ml), sesame seeds (32g, pre-ground if not using high-speed blender), soy sauce (2 tbsp), miso (2 tbsp), rice vinegar (1 tbsp), and white pepper (1/4 tsp) to the blender. Blend until completely smooth and creamy. Taste — it should be quite savoury and salty. Add the remaining 2 tsp soy sauce if needed.
8. Pour the blended broth into the saucepan (the one used to cook Udon noodles) and bring gradually to a gentle simmer over medium-low heat, whisking frequently — do not increase the heat or it will bubble over. Simmer for 4-6 min. until hot. If the broth is too thick, whisk in more mushroom broth (up to another 240ml) until you reach your preferred consistency.

### Assemble
9. Add ~1/4 of the noodles to a large bowl, whisk the hot broth and pour over the noodles. Top with crispy mushrooms and reserved green parts of the spring onions. Finish with a drizzle of toasted sesame oil and chilli crisp or fresh chilli if desired.
