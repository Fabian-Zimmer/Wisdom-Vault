## Recommended Equipment
- Dutch oven, deep sauté pan, saucier, or even large wok — for the risotto; use a non-stick if possible, otherwise use more oil to prevent sticking
- Medium saucepan — for keeping the broth warm
- Large frying pan — for the mushrooms; keep separate from the risotto pan so mushrooms don't go mushy

## Instructions
**Notes:** 
- Keep the mushrooms and risotto cooking separately, because cooking mushrooms with the rice makes them mushy and makes the risotto harder to stir. 
- Hot broth is essential: cold broth cools the rice, slows cooking, and leads to uneven texture. 
- Don't salt mushrooms at the start, because it draws out water and makes them less crispy and meaty; add salt only towards the end of cooking. 
- Risotto is best served immediately; it loses its creamy texture as it sits.

1. Take the vegan butter (56g) and miso paste (30-35g) out of the fridge and leave to soften. Once soft, use a fork to cream them together in a small bowl until well combined and whipped. Set aside.
2. Cook the mushrooms: heat a large frying pan over medium-high heat with olive oil (1 tbsp). Once shimmering, add mushrooms and cook undisturbed for a few minutes to develop browning, then stir occasionally for a total of 8-9 min. until nicely browned.
3. Reduce heat to medium. Add thyme (2 tbsp) and half the garlic (3 cloves) to the mushrooms. Add salt (1/2 tsp) now — not before, to keep mushrooms crispy and meaty. Cook for 2-4 min., stirring frequently to prevent the garlic from burning.
4. Add the miso butter and stir to coat the mushrooms, cooking for 2 more min. Season with a pinch of salt and pepper. Set aside.
5. Heat the vegetable broth (1.5-1.9L) in a saucepan and bring to a rapid simmer, then reduce to a gentle simmer to keep it hot throughout cooking.
6. Clean the leeks: peel away papery outer layers, cut once lengthwise, then slice into thin half-rings, then:
	- for thorough wash: submerge in a bowl of cold water, swishing with your hands to loosen the dirt. Scoop out with your hands or a slotted spoon, leaving the sand behind. Dry on a towel.
	- for rinse wash: rinse in a strainer and shake out excess water.
7. In the Dutch oven or deep sauté pan, heat olive oil (2 tbsp) over medium heat. Add leeks and remaining garlic (3 cloves) and cook for 2-3 min. until just softened.
8. Add the rice (370g) and stir quickly for 60-90 seconds until the grains are well-coated and smell slightly toasty — this helps the rice absorb liquid slowly without becoming soggy.
9. Deglaze:
	- With alcohol: pour in the wine (160ml) and stir to scrape up any browned bits. Cook for 3-4 min. until mostly evaporated and no longer smells of alcohol.
	- Without alcohol: skip directly to next broth-adding step, but add a splash of white wine vinegar with first broth ladle.
10. Ladle in 1 cup (240ml) of hot broth and stir frequently — roughly every 30 seconds — but not constantly (constant stirring leads to gluey rice). Once absorbed, add the next 1-2 cups of broth. Repeat for ~20 min. until the rice is slightly firm, creamy, but not mushy. Use the smear test: place a grain on a flat surface (e.g., a cutting board) and smear downward with your finger — it should be mostly smooth but still show a small al dente white centre.
11. Add the cooked mushrooms to the risotto and stir to warm through for 1-2 min.
12. Remove from heat. Stir in (vegan) parmesan (30g) if using. Taste and adjust salt and pepper. Garnish with chopped parsley and serve immediately.
