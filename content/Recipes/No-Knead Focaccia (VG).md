## Recommended Equipment
- Large bowl — for the dough
- Baking pan — metal tin preferred over glass (heats more evenly); 8×16 inch or similar
- Plastic wrap — for the second rise (a tea towel risks sticking to the oily dough)
- Wire rack — for cooling

## Ingredients

### Dough
| Amount   | Name           | Prep/Notes                                                                                      |
| -------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 325g     | Bread flour    | Bread flour strongly preferred over all-purpose — more protein = better gluten without kneading |
| 300ml    | Warm water     | 30-35°C; yeast dies above ~58°C                                                                 |
| 4g       | Active dry yeast |                                                                                                 |
| 5g       | Sugar          | Helps activate and feed the yeast                                                               |
| 5g       | Salt           |                                                                                                 |
|          | Olive oil      | Generous amount — for the bowl, the pan, and your hands; use good quality                       |

### Toppings
| Amount | Name           | Prep/Notes                        |
| ------ | -------------- | --------------------------------- |
|        | Flaky sea salt |                                   |
|        | Fresh rosemary |                                   |
|        | Olive oil      | For dimpling and drizzling on top |

## Instructions
**Notes:**
- This is a high-hydration dough (~92%) — it will be very wet and sticky throughout. This is expected and necessary for the fluffy, airy result.
- The cold overnight fermentation is what develops the gluten instead of kneading — don't rush this step.
- Metal tin pans are preferred over glass — they heat faster and produce crispier edges.
- Focaccia is a forgiving bread — even if slightly overproofed, it will still bake well.

### Day Before
1. In a large bowl, combine warm water (300ml), yeast (4g), and sugar (5g). Stir and leave for ~5 min. until bubbly and active — this confirms the yeast is alive.
2. Add bread flour (325g) and salt (5g). Mix with a spoon until you get a shaggy dough, then use oiled hands to bring it together.
3. Leave to rest for 10 min., then perform a set of stretch and folds: grab the dough, stretch it up and fold it over, rotate the bowl 90° and repeat — 4 folds total per set. You'll feel it tighten and become more structured.
4. Rest for another 10 min., then do one more set of stretch and folds. Shape loosely into a ball, seam-side down.
5. Coat the bowl with a thin layer of olive oil, place the dough in it, and coat it in the oil. Cover with a damp tea towel or cling film and refrigerate for 12-18h.
 
### Baking Day
6. Remove the dough from the fridge. If there are any very large bubbles on the surface, gently pop them — smaller bubbles are fine and desirable.
7. Coat the baking pan generously with olive oil. Tip the dough out into the pan. Leave to rest for ~10 min. so the gluten relaxes, then use oiled hands to gently stretch it to the edges. If it resists, wait a few more minutes and try again.
8. Cover loosely with plastic wrap (not a tea towel — it will stick to the oily dough) and leave to rise at room temperature for ~1h until roughly 1.5 times bigger and visibly jiggly and bubbly.
9. Preheat oven to 220°C. Drizzle a little more olive oil over the surface, then dimple the dough all the way to the bottom with oiled fingers — don't be too aggressive or you'll lose the bubbles. Scatter flaky sea salt and rosemary over the top.
10. Bake on the centre rack for 25-30 min. until deeply golden on top with crispy edges.
11. Leave to cool on a wire rack for at least 20 min. before slicing.
