`|What this book is about|`
This book concerns a single idea, a single principle, that is in my estimation connected to everything else, all other thoughts, ideas, and experiences. In a way that will become evident later, it can be thought of the single root cause for all of reality and all that it apparently entails: our physical universe, our experiences, the self, purpose and meaning of life, etc.

`|Open-minded scepticism|`
The mindset that brought me this far is one of open-minded scepticism. By being open-minded one can begin any journey on the infinite ocean of experience, and with scepticism you can navigate and course correct so as not to get stuck at islands of belief, whirlpools of ego, and deep waters of fear. These three concepts of belief, ego, and fear will be of importance later. I only wish of you, dear reader, that you adopt a similar mindset as you advance through these pages.

`|Skippable part of this book|`
This grand principle that sources reality as I understand it is discussed in Part 2 of the book. Part 1 gives an epistemological tour and is therefore a philosophical discussion on how we get to know things, the limitations of such knowledge, and how to integrate that into our human experiences. It is a rather stand-alone part, and may therefore be skipped if one wishes to get to the core ideas of the book.

`|Citations, influences|`
This book is based on a whole lot of other literature (and other content/media I studied), so to cite and link works for individual statements, ideas, or chapter would be an enormous amount of work (and I'm unsure if possible at all, or what it would give the reader). Therefore, I do it like in GEB, where all works are listed in the back of the book, with a star or double-star mark for works that (heavily) influenced my thinking. Individual quotes do indeed (if I could trace it to the original source correctly) have a citation.

Probably all things discussed are perennial, **time-honored questions** and discussions. It is always worth to revisit them, however, as our knowledge pool increases and methods of science and thinking evolve and can give us new perspectives and ideas.

`|Gathering info, easier to go wrong than right|`
It's very hard to find good ideas, meaningful concepts and solutions, etc. To think one can generate or find them all yourself is arrogant. Suppose 10 people have thought long about reality and each came up with 1 good thought they write about. It's much more optimal to go out there and read about thoughts of others that they have evaluated to be of merit and worth to write about. It's similar to the fundamental truth that it's much more likely, in an exploration of states in a virtually infinite state-space, to find things that are "false" and "go wrong" rather than the "truths" and "working" things, so is it with your own thoughts and ideas.
