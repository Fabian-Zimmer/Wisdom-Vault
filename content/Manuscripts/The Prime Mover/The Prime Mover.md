
Certain things seem to be apparent, at least to our human lens for experience:
1. There exists something rather than nothing. 
2. This something is changing rather than static. 
3. There is an infinite variety of "parts" that each seems to be some sort of agent that is integrated in the nature of this changing "system".

By infinite variety I mean to say, that we can identify "parts" of the "system", with scope and nature depending on our intentions. Is our intention to talk about the biology of the human body? Then it is useful to identify parts as cells together with their contents and interactions. But if our intention is to chart migration patterns across nations, then individual humans seem to be the parts of choice.

Parts and system are very useful notions for the prime mover principle, but I want to remind that by no means is this to be interpreted literally, that reality is some mechanical system comprised of various parts. It simply serves as the first iteration of metaphorical imagery, and more iterations will follow.

Now, the principle is...

the only way to escape this determinism=non-existence dead-end is to allow the "move" of each agentic part of the system to be utterly and entirely independent of any other influence/move/part of the system. I.e., that it is a free move by the part ("free will"...).
